Almost every testing laboratory in Pakistan describes itself as accredited. Some are, for some tests. Some were, and have lapsed. Some have copied the wording, and occasionally the certificate number, from a laboratory that is. If a report is going to a regulator, a bank, an export buyer or a court, the person relying on it needs a way to check the claim in a few minutes rather than take it on trust. This guide sets out how to verify a PNAC accredited laboratory in Pakistan, what the accreditation number and scope actually tell you, and the signs that a claim is not what it seems. We use Envi Tech AL's own accreditations as the worked example, because they are the ones we can show.

Key takeaways

  • The Pakistan National Accreditation Council (PNAC) is the national accreditation body. It accredits testing and calibration laboratories to ISO/IEC 17025 and publishes a directory of accredited laboratories with their scopes.
  • Each accredited laboratory has a certificate number in the form LAB-nnn. Envi Tech AL holds LAB-285 for its Karachi laboratory and LAB-347 for its Lahore laboratory.
  • Accreditation is granted to a specific location for a specific scope: matrix, parameter, method and range. It does not transfer to another branch, another matrix or another method.
  • Verification takes three steps: find the laboratory in the PNAC directory, read the scope document, and ask the laboratory for its current certificate. Directory listings can lag behind a renewal, so the certificate settles the date.
  • A laboratory that is accredited for drinking water chemistry is not, by that fact, accredited for microbiology, stack emissions or soil. Ask about the parameter you need.

What PNAC accreditation is, and what it is not

PNAC operates under the Ministry of Science and Technology and is Pakistan's signatory to the international mutual recognition arrangements for laboratory accreditation. When PNAC accredits a laboratory to ISO/IEC 17025:2017, it has assessed that laboratory's technical competence for the tests in its scope: staff qualifications, validated methods, calibrated equipment with traceability, quality control on each batch, participation in proficiency testing, record keeping and impartiality. Assessors visit the laboratory, witness tests, review records, and return for surveillance visits during the accreditation cycle.

Three things accreditation is not. It is not a licence to operate; a laboratory can be registered and operating without it. It is not a certification of the laboratory's management system in the way ISO 9001 is; ISO/IEC 17025 includes management requirements but its purpose is technical competence. And it is not blanket approval. The accreditation attaches to a scope, and nothing outside the scope is covered by it.

Reading the accreditation number and scope

A PNAC laboratory certificate carries a number, LAB-nnn, the laboratory's name and address, the standard (ISO/IEC 17025:2017), the field (testing, calibration or medical), the issue date and the validity date. The number is tied to the address. Envi Tech AL's Karachi laboratory in Bahadurabad is LAB-285; the Lahore laboratory in Johar Town is LAB-347. A report issued from Lahore cites LAB-347, not LAB-285, and a parameter accredited at one site may not be accredited at the other.

The scope is a separate document, usually several pages, and it is the part most people never read. Each line names a matrix (drinking water, wastewater, ambient air, stack emissions, soil), a parameter (arsenic, BOD5, total suspended particulate, noise level), the method (an APHA Standard Methods reference, an ASTM, EPA or ISO method number, or an in-house method identifier) and often a measurement range. A laboratory can be accredited for arsenic in drinking water by atomic absorption spectrometry and not for arsenic in soil, or for BOD5 but not COD. When a buyer or regulator asks for accredited results, they mean results for parameters that appear on that list, at that site, by that method.

Three steps to verify a laboratory

  1. Find the laboratory in the PNAC directory. PNAC publishes a list of accredited conformity assessment bodies on its website, pnac.gov.pk, searchable by name, city and field. Confirm the laboratory name, the address and the certificate number match the report in front of you. A laboratory that does not appear at all, or appears under a different address, needs an explanation before you rely on its report.
  2. Open the scope. The directory entry links to the laboratory's scope of accreditation. Search it for the matrix and parameter you care about. If your parameter is not there, the result may still be sound, but it is not an accredited result.
  3. Ask the laboratory for the current certificate. The directory is updated periodically and can show an earlier validity date for a laboratory that has since renewed. Envi Tech AL's Karachi certificate, for instance, was renewed on reassessment and now runs to 2029, while an older validity date may still be visible in some listings. The certificate the laboratory sends you, with the reassessment date, is the current position. An accredited laboratory will send it the same day; hesitation is itself an answer.

Signs that a claim is not what it seems

Most problems are not forgery. They are loose wording, lapsed status, or scope that does not cover the test. The signs below are the ones we see most often when a client brings a competing report for a second opinion.

  • "ISO 17025 certified" with no number. Accreditation to ISO/IEC 17025 comes with a certificate number and a scope. A claim with neither is marketing, not accreditation.
  • "ISO 9001 certified laboratory" offered as if it were the same thing. ISO 9001 is a management system certificate. It says nothing about the competence of a particular test.
  • A LAB number that belongs to someone else. Check the number in the directory. If it resolves to a different name or address, the report is citing another laboratory's accreditation. We have seen our own numbers reproduced on other websites; the directory entry is how a reader tells the two apart.
  • Accreditation claimed for a branch that is not on the certificate. A head office certificate does not extend to a collection centre or a new city. Each site is assessed separately.
  • Every parameter on the report shown as accredited. Scopes are specific. A laboratory that marks a hundred parameters across water, air, soil and noise as accredited has either an unusually broad scope, which the directory will confirm, or is not reading its own scope.
  • "In process" or "applied for" presented as accredited. Application is not accreditation. The laboratory may be competent, but its results are not accredited until the certificate is issued.

Why the distinction matters for your report

Sindh EPA and Punjab EPA ask for environmental monitoring data from certified laboratories, and both agencies maintain their own lists of approved laboratories alongside PNAC accreditation. Export buyers, especially in textiles, food and pharmaceuticals, increasingly ask for ISO/IEC 17025 accredited results for the parameters in their specification, and their auditors check scopes. A bank financing a plant will ask for the same. In each case the question is not "is the laboratory accredited?" but "is this parameter, from this site, by this method, within the accreditation?". A report that answers that question on its face, with the number, the method and the scope mark per parameter, saves everyone the argument.

Frequently asked questions

How do I check whether a laboratory in Pakistan is PNAC accredited?

Search the directory of accredited laboratories on pnac.gov.pk for the laboratory name or certificate number, confirm the address matches, open the scope document and check the parameter you need, then ask the laboratory for its current certificate to confirm the validity date.

What is the difference between ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation and ISO 9001 certification?

ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation is an assessment of a laboratory's technical competence for specific tests, granted by an accreditation body such as PNAC. ISO 9001 certification is an audit of a management system by a certification body. A laboratory can hold both; only the first speaks to the reliability of a test result.

Does PNAC accreditation cover all tests a laboratory offers?

No. It covers the matrices, parameters and methods listed in the laboratory's scope document, at the accredited site. Tests outside the scope can still be performed and reported, but they are not accredited results.

What are Envi Tech AL's PNAC accreditation numbers?

LAB-285 for the Karachi laboratory in Bahadurabad and LAB-347 for the Lahore laboratory in Johar Town. Each applies only to that site and its published scope.

The PNAC website shows an older expiry date for a laboratory. Has it lapsed?

Not necessarily. Listings are updated periodically and can lag a renewal. Ask the laboratory for the current certificate showing the reassessment and validity dates, and compare the certificate number with the directory.

Does Sindh EPA or Punjab EPA require PNAC accreditation?

Both agencies maintain their own lists of certified or approved environmental laboratories, and PNAC accreditation is a central part of qualifying for them. For a regulatory submission, check that the laboratory appears on the relevant agency's list as well as in the PNAC directory.

Can a laboratory's accreditation be used by its branch in another city?

No. Accreditation is site-specific. A second site needs its own assessment and certificate, which is why Envi Tech AL holds separate numbers for Karachi and Lahore.
