Most people choose a water testing company the way they choose a courier: whoever answers the phone first and quotes the lowest number. That works until the report is read by someone who matters, a Sindh EPA inspector, an export buyer's auditor, a bank's due diligence team, or a court, and the questions start. Which method? Within which accreditation scope? Who sampled it, and when? This guide sets out how to choose a water testing company or environmental testing laboratory in Pakistan so that the report holds up when those questions come. It applies equally to drinking water, effluent, air and noise work, and it is written from the inside: Envi Tech AL runs an environmental testing lab in Karachi and an environmental testing lab in Lahore, and these are the questions we would want a client to ask us.

Key takeaways

  • Start from the purpose of the report. A regulator, a buyer, a bank and a household each need different things from the same sample, and the right laboratory is the one whose scope and reporting match that purpose.
  • Accreditation is specific. Ask whether the parameters you need are within the laboratory's PNAC scope at the site that will test them, not whether the company "is accredited".
  • Sampling decides the result more often than analysis does. A company that offers field sampling, chain of custody and holding-time control is selling you a defensible result, not a number.
  • Price comparisons only work parameter for parameter, method for method. A cheaper quote with a different method or fewer parameters is a different product.
  • Verify before you rely: the PNAC directory, the EPA certified laboratory lists, and the laboratory's own report verification route.

1. Decide what the report is for

The purpose fixes everything downstream: the parameters, the standard the results are compared with, the sampling method, and who must be able to verify the report. Before contacting anyone, write the answer to one question: who will read this report, and what will they do with it?

A company that asks you this question before quoting is already doing part of the job. One that quotes a flat "water test" price without asking is pricing a product that may not be the one you need.

2. Check accreditation the way an auditor does

Almost every laboratory in Pakistan describes itself as accredited or certified. The useful question is narrower: is the parameter I need, in the matrix I have, by the method you will use, within your PNAC scope at the laboratory that will run it? Accreditation under ISO/IEC 17025 is granted by the Pakistan National Accreditation Council to a specific site for a specific scope. A laboratory accredited for drinking water chemistry in Karachi is not, by that fact, accredited for microbiology, for effluent, or for its branch in another city.

Three checks take about ten minutes and are explained step by step in our guide to verifying a PNAC accredited laboratory: find the laboratory and its LAB number in the PNAC directory, open the scope document and look for your parameter, and ask for the current certificate because the directory can lag a renewal. For regulatory work, add a fourth: confirm the laboratory appears on the Sindh EPA or Punjab EPA list of certified laboratories, which is a separate approval from PNAC accreditation.

Envi Tech AL's numbers are LAB-285 for Karachi and LAB-347 for Lahore. Each applies to that site and its published scope, and our reports mark, parameter by parameter, which results fall inside it.

3. Ask who takes the sample, and how

Two laboratories can analyse the same water perfectly and report different numbers, because the samples were not the same. Microbiology that waited 30 hours in a hot car, a metals sample in an unpreserved bottle, a pH measured at the bench rather than the tap: each produces a tidy number that describes the bottle rather than the water. The questions to ask a water testing company are therefore about the sample, not the instrument.

  • Does the laboratory supply the correct bottles and preservatives for each parameter, or expect you to find them?
  • Will the laboratory's own team sample on site, with calibrated field meters, and record the point, time and conditions? For any report going to a regulator or auditor, this is the answer you want.
  • Is there a chain-of-custody form, and does the report cite it?
  • Are holding times stated, and will a result analysed outside its holding time be flagged on the report?

4. Compare quotes parameter for parameter

Water testing quotes in Pakistan range widely, and most of the spread is explained by what is inside them rather than by margin. Before comparing prices, put the quotes side by side and check five things.

  1. Parameter list. A "drinking water panel" may contain 8 parameters or 25. Compare the lists, not the labels.
  2. Method per parameter. Arsenic by a field kit and arsenic by atomic absorption spectrometry are different tests with different detection limits and different standing in front of a regulator.
  3. Accredited or not. Some laboratories price accredited and non-accredited results differently, and some quote non-accredited results without saying so.
  4. Sampling and collection. Included, extra, or not offered.
  5. Reporting. Limits shown beside results, a verification route, and the report format your reader expects.

A quote that is 30 percent lower because it omits microbiology, uses a screening method for metals, or leaves sampling to you is a different product at a different price, not a better deal on the same one. Our guide to water testing costs in Karachi explains what drives the cost of each panel.

5. Look at the report before you order

Ask for a sample report with the client details removed. A good one answers your reader's questions on its face: the sample identity and receipt record; for each parameter the result, unit, method, reporting limit and the standard it is compared with; a clear mark on anything outside the accreditation scope or holding time; the accreditation number for the site; and an authorised signature with a way to verify the document. If you have to phone the laboratory to find out which method was used, the report is incomplete. Our guide to reading a water test report shows a full example.

6. Check the verification route

Test reports are copied, altered and occasionally invented. A laboratory that takes its own reports seriously gives the reader a way to check them independently: a report number and date that can be entered on a verification page, or a direct confirmation route. Envi Tech AL reports can be checked on the report verification portal. If a company cannot tell you how a third party would confirm a report is genuine, assume your reader will ask the same question.

7. Weigh location and turnaround honestly

Proximity matters for one reason: holding times. Microbiology must be analysed within 24 hours of sampling, so a laboratory your courier reaches overnight, or whose field team can visit, is worth more for bacteriology than a distant one with a better brochure. For chemistry, most parameters travel for days when cooled and preserved, so the nearest laboratory is not automatically the best one. Ask for a reporting date at booking rather than a general turnaround promise, and treat a laboratory that will not commit to a date with caution.

8. Signs to walk away

  • Accreditation claimed with no LAB number, or a number that resolves in the PNAC directory to a different name or address.
  • "ISO 9001 certified" offered as the answer to a question about test competence.
  • Any promise about the outcome of the test. A laboratory reports what is measured; the result belongs to the water, not to the quotation.
  • No sampling guidance, no bottles, no chain-of-custody form.
  • A report with no methods, no limits and no way to verify it.
  • Prices that cannot be broken down by parameter.

A short checklist for the phone call

  1. Here is what the report is for. Which parameters do you recommend, and against which standard?
  2. Which of those are within your PNAC scope at the laboratory that will test them? What is the LAB number?
  3. Are you on the Sindh EPA or Punjab EPA certified laboratory list?
  4. Who samples, with what bottles, and how is chain of custody recorded?
  5. What is the method for each parameter, and the reporting limit?
  6. When will I receive the report, and how can my auditor verify it?

Frequently asked questions

How do I choose a water testing company in Pakistan?

Decide what the report is for, check that the parameters you need are within the laboratory's PNAC scope at the testing site, confirm EPA certification for regulatory work, ask who samples and how chain of custody is kept, compare quotes parameter by parameter, and look at a sample report before ordering.

What is the difference between an accredited and a certified laboratory?

Accreditation (ISO/IEC 17025, granted by PNAC) is an assessment of technical competence for specific tests. Certification by Sindh EPA or Punjab EPA is the provincial agency's approval to produce monitoring data for its submissions. Regulatory work usually needs both; ISO 9001 certification is neither.

Is the cheapest water test good enough?

Sometimes. For a quick check of TDS and hardness on a borewell, a basic panel is fine. For anything a regulator, buyer or bank will read, the method, the accreditation scope and the sampling record decide whether the report is accepted, and those are what cheaper quotes usually leave out.

Should the laboratory take the sample?

For regulatory, audit and legal purposes, yes. Sampling by the laboratory's field team, recorded on a chain-of-custody form, is what makes the result defensible. For a household check, careful self-sampling in the laboratory's bottles is acceptable.

How can I check a test report is genuine?

Use the laboratory's verification route. Envi Tech AL reports are checked on the report verification portal using the report number and date. A report that cannot be verified independently should not be relied on.

Does the laboratory need to be in my city?

Only for microbiology and other short holding-time parameters, which need a same-day or overnight route or a field visit. Chemistry and metals travel for days when preserved and cooled, so a laboratory in Karachi or Lahore can serve any city in Pakistan by courier.
